About Folk Calm

A digital archive preserving the quiet, everyday traditions of Chinese households — not as medical advice, but as cultural memory.

Why This Site Exists

I started Folk Calm because these traditions deserve to be written down before they fade. Not because they're "correct" in any clinical sense — they're not medical advice and never will be — but because they tell a story about how ordinary people have sought comfort, warmth, and rhythm in daily life across centuries. The gentle domestic customs that have traveled through Chinese families for generations — warm foot soaks, kitchen teas, slow evening rituals — are the kind of things grandparents pass to grandchildren without ever writing down. I'm writing them down.

Research Methodology

All content published on Folk Calm draws from three sources:

  • Oral history interviews — conversations with elders from various regions of China who describe the household practices they grew up with
  • Classical Chinese texts — historical documents such as the Huangdi Neijing (Yellow Emperor's Inner Classic) and Shennong Ben Cao Jing (Divine Farmer's Materia Medica), cited as cultural context, never as clinical authority
  • Published ethnographies — academic studies of Chinese domestic life and folk customs, cross-referenced for accuracy

What We Are Not

Folk Calm is not a source of medical, health, or lifestyle advice. We do not make claims about what any practice will do for your body, your health, or your wellbeing. We describe what people have traditionally done in Chinese households — nothing more. If you are considering adopting any practice described on this site, please consult a qualified professional who knows your personal circumstances.

Who I Am

I'm a cultural researcher and writer with a deep respect for Chinese folk traditions — particularly the quiet, domestic ones that don't make it into textbooks. I work with native speakers and regional informants to make sure the practices I document are represented accurately and with attention to how much they vary from place to place.

This site is a personal project. I write it, I maintain it, and I stand behind it. If you have corrections, suggestions, or family stories you'd like to share, I'd genuinely love to hear from you — just drop a note to contact@tcmwellness.xyz.
